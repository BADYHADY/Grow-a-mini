The inspiration is the simplicity and progression structure of Roblox games such as Grow a Chicken Fighter, Grow a Garden, Grow a Garden 2, Steal a Brainrot, 99 Nights in the Forest and other successful Roblox progression/social experiences.

However:

## WE ARE NOT MAKING A CLONE.

Our unique fantasy is:

# YOUR ROBLOX AVATAR BECOMES YOUR FIGHTER.

When a player joins the experience, the game generates a miniature version of their Roblox avatar.

Their little avatar becomes a collectible/evolving fighter.

Imagine seeing:

# 2. CURRENT RESEARCH BASELINE

Use this only as a starting hypothesis and verify it again.

Grow a Chicken Fighter currently demonstrates a highly understandable progression structure:

Hatch → Fight → Cash → Tower → Fuse → Rebirth → Offline progress.

Grow a Garden-style games demonstrate:

Acquire → Grow → Wait → Discover rarity → Sell/upgrade → Flex → Return later.

Grow a Garden 2 adds strong social systems including guild competition and nighttime stealing.

Steal a Brainrot demonstrates how powerful a ridiculously simple social interaction can become:

Acquire → Generate → Protect → Steal → Flex → Rebirth.

99 Nights demonstrates that active cooperative gameplay and a sentence-level fantasy can support enormous engagement:

“Build a camp with friends. Something is watching you.”

Our game needs that same level of conceptual clarity.

The player should be able to describe MINI ME FIGHTERS as:

> “You grow a tiny version of your Roblox avatar into an insane fighter.”

That is the elevator pitch.

# 3. CORE DESIGN RULE

If a 10-year-old needs a tutorial paragraph to understand the basic objective, simplify it.

The game should work at three levels:

### 5 seconds

“I have a tiny version of myself.”

### 30 seconds

“My mini fights things and becomes stronger.”

“I want to evolve him and see what he turns into.”

“My fighter trained while I was gone and there is something new to collect.”

### Next week

“I want an insane Mini collection, higher Ascension, leaderboard rank, rare mutations and limited event variants.”

# 7. THE MINI SYSTEM

This is our killer feature.

Use Roblox's avatar systems such as HumanoidDescription rather than manually attempting to recreate avatars.

The Mini should preserve important identity:

- avatar head
- face
- hair
- accessories where technically appropriate
- clothing
- colors
- recognizable silhouette

But transform proportions to create a cute/funny collectible figure.

Think:

Roblox avatar × collectible figurine × anime fighter.

Potential proportions:

- larger head
- shorter limbs
- chunky hands
- exaggerated feet
- slightly cartoony proportions

It should look intentionally designed, not simply Scale = 0.3.

Build a robust MiniAvatarService.

Requirements:

- safely retrieve avatar appearance
- cache descriptions
- handle failed avatar loading
- handle layered clothing
- handle large accessories
- blacklist visually broken accessories where necessary
- support R6/R15 considerations
- support NPC cloning
- support many minis efficiently
- optimize network replication
- LOD/distance optimizations
- never trust client-supplied UserIds for privileged operations

# 10. THE FIGHTING SYSTEM

Grow a Chicken Fighter has passive-fighter appeal.

Our version should provide slightly more player agency without becoming complicated.

Design combat in layers.

## Casual player

Can allow Mini to fight largely automatically.

## Engaged player

Can influence combat using:

- positioning
- one active special
- dodge
- target selection
- rage/ultimate timing

## Advanced player

Can optimize:

- build
- traits
- abilities
- gear
- synergies
- boss timing
- team composition

The controls must work perfectly on mobile.

Never require 12 keybinds.

Initial design:

AUTO ATTACK

+

ONE ACTIVE ABILITY

+

ONE ULTIMATE

potentially later:

DODGE.

The Mini should feel like a creature/fighter you command rather than the player directly being the combat model.
